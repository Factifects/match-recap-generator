### SCENE 6

**Scene Type:** Bar Chart

**Narration:** There's a second memory effect layered on top, and it's not about routine at all — it's about age itself. Decades of memory research find the same pattern again and again: ask people for their most vivid, most tellable life memories, and they cluster overwhelmingly in the teenage and early-twenties years, not childhood, not middle age. Psychologists call it the reminiscence bump — it's thought to be the period your brain is busiest working out who you are.

### SCENE 12

**Scene Type:** Canvas

**Narration:** There's a third piece to this, right down at the level of biology. A 2019 study out of Duke University proposed that as brains age, the neural pathways carrying visual information get a little longer, a little less efficient — so the brain quite literally acquires new mental snapshots of the world more slowly than it used to. Run the same real ten seconds through a brain sampling fewer snapshots, and it will always feel like it went by quicker — the same trick as watching footage shot at a lower frame rate.
